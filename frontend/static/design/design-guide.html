SciStarter Design Guide

How to make a page built in WordPress, or any other visual editor, look like part of SciStarter.

This guide gives every rule as a plain value: hex codes for colors, pixels for sizes. Type those values into your editor's color, typography and spacing settings. Click any value in a box to copy it, ready to paste into your editor.

The essentials

If you remember nothing else:

  • Body text Inter, , line height , in Charcoal .
  • Headings Bold. Aqua on white, Indigo on tinted backgrounds. Barlow for H1 and H2, Inter for H3 and H4.
  • Links and buttons Link Teal , darkening to Deep Teal on hover. Nothing else is teal.
  • Spacing between related things, inside panels and between columns of text, between sections.
  • Width Keep paragraphs to wide or less. Long lines are hard to read.
  • Contrast Before you put text on a colored background, check the pair in the combinations table.

Rules for text

  • One Heading 1 per page: the page title. Sections start at Heading 2. Don't skip levels, for example going from Heading 2 straight to Heading 4. Screen readers and search engines use the levels as the page outline.
  • Choose a heading level for its place in the outline, not its size. To make text stand out, use bold, not a heading.
  • Aqua headings only on white or Indigo. On any tinted background (Sky Blue, Off-White, gray), use Indigo for headings. Aqua is too light there (see combinations).
  • Left-align paragraphs. Center only headings and short intros of one or two lines. Never justify text.
  • Don't underline text that isn't a link, and don't color it teal. Readers will try to click it.
  • Use capitals only for short labels above a heading (see eyebrow labels). Never write sentences in capitals.
  • Body text is . Don't set reading text smaller than . Use only for credits and fine print.

Colors

Pick only from these colors. If your editor has a custom palette, add these colors to it and remove the others.

Blues

  • Aqua primary
    The SciStarter blue: logo, headings, decorative shapes and backgrounds. As text, only at heading sizes.
  • Indigo primary-dark
    Dark backgrounds and banners. Headings on tinted backgrounds.
  • Link Teal primary-action
    Links and buttons only, so that teal always means “you can click this.”
  • Deep Teal primary-action-dark
    Hover color for links and buttons. Links on tinted backgrounds.
  • Sky Blue primary-light
    Background for highlighted sections and callout boxes. Never use it for text.
  • Line Blue linework
    1px borders around cards and boxes, and divider lines.

Coral

  • Coral accent1
    The accent: logo, underlines, icons, and the second color when a chart or diagram needs two. Never for text, and never behind white text.
  • Coral Action accent1-action
    Coral darkened just enough to carry white text. Background for a single attention-grabbing button.
  • Brick accent1-dark
    Coral as text on white, error messages, and hover for Coral Action buttons.

Neutrals

  • White white
    The page background. Text on Indigo and on buttons.
  • Off-White neutral-xlight
    Background for quiet, alternating sections.
  • Light Gray neutral-light
    Dividers and inactive or disabled things.
  • Mid Gray neutral-mid
    The lightest gray that can be used for text (captions, secondary details), and only on white.
  • Charcoal neutral-dark
    Body text. Softer than black and still easy to read.
  • Black black
    Rarely needed. Prefer Charcoal for text.

Color combinations

Text must stand out clearly from its background, or many readers can't read it: people with low vision, people with color blindness, and anyone reading a phone outdoors. The measure is the contrast ratio, from 1:1 (invisible) to 21:1 (black on white).

  • Any text (4.5:1 or more): fine for everything, including body text.
  • Large text (3:1 or more): only for text at least 24px, or bold text at least 18.7px. On this site that means headings, not paragraphs or buttons.
  • Avoid (under 3:1): don't use this pair for text at all.

Links and buttons

Text links

Links are Link Teal with no underline. On hover they turn Deep Teal and gain an underline. On tinted and dark backgrounds, follow the section styles above.

  • Link text should say where the link goes: “Read the volunteer guide,” not “click here” or “learn more.” Many people skim the links alone.
  • Link a few meaningful words, not a whole sentence or a bare web address.

Spacing and layout

Spacing steps

SciStarter uses four standard spacing sizes, each twice the size of the one before. Use them wherever your editor asks for padding, margin, gap or spacing.

Step Size Use it for
Small Inside small things: the space above and below button text, around tags and labels.
Half Between closely related things: a heading and its text, paragraphs, list items. The gap between cards in a grid. Padding inside cards.
Standard Padding inside panels and colored sections. The gap between columns of text.
Double Between the major sections of a page, and above a heading that follows text.

Page widths

Content uses one of three widths. In WordPress, set the theme's content width to the reading width and its wide width to the wide width. Blocks then offer exactly these three choices.

Width Size Use it for
Reading width Paragraphs, lists, and images that go with the text. Wider lines are tiring to read.
Wide width Grids of cards, columns, wide images: anything that isn't running text.
Full width The whole screen Background colors and banner images only. Keep the text and buttons inside such a band within the wide width.

The wide width is the page's widest content, , less a page edge on each side.

The space beside a column. A reading-width or wide-width column sits in the middle of the screen. The space on each side of it, which many people call the margin, isn't something you set: it's whatever the screen has left over, so it changes from one screen to the next.

So don't make a column narrower by giving it left and right margins or padding. Those only look right at the screen width you set them on. Choose the column's width and center it, and the space on each side takes care of itself. On a screen narrower than the column, the column fills the screen except for the page edges.

  • No more than four columns. On phones, every layout becomes a single column.
  • Order matters. When columns stack, the left column goes on top. Put the main content on the left and the supporting material on the right. A menu that controls the content is the one exception.
  • Line up the tops of columns and cards in a row, and give cards in the same row the same height.
  • Space columns apart with your editor's gap setting, not with margins on the columns. The gap goes only between columns, so the outer edges of the row still line up with the text above and below it.

Screen sizes

The layout changes at a few fixed screen widths, called breakpoints. Many editors, WordPress included, can preview a page at tablet and phone sizes. Check every page at each size before you publish. Many visitors arrive on a phone.

Screen width Typically What changes
Under 640px Phone Everything is one column. Headings use their smaller phone sizes. Page edges are . The menu folds into a menu button.
640 to 767px Large phone, small tablet Card grids can go two across, and cards with an image start to put it beside their text.
768 to 1023px Tablet The main change. Columns sit side by side, headings reach full size, page edges grow to , and the full menu bar appears.
1024 to 1279px Laptop Sidebars and menus move beside the main content. Card grids go three across.
1280px and up Desktop Nothing new: the page widens until it reaches its widest content, , and then stays centered.

Images fill the width of their column on phones, so anything at the edges of a wide banner becomes very small there.

Images

  • Upload images at twice the size they appear, so they stay sharp on high-resolution screens. An image at wide width appears up to wide, so make it wide. A full-width banner can be wider still on big screens, so make it at least that. An image in a column of text appears up to , so make it wide.
  • Banners work best about three times wider than tall (for example 2400px × 800px). Keep faces and key details away from the edges, which get cropped or become tiny on phones.
  • Card images are landscape, twice as wide as tall (for example 800px × 400px).
  • Photos as JPEG or WebP, compressed. A banner should rarely be more than about 500 KB. Logos and graphics as SVG or PNG with a transparent background.
  • Don't put text in images. It can't be read on phones, by screen readers or by search engines. Write it as real text next to the image.
  • Corners are square, except images at the top of a card, which follow the card's corner radius.
  • Logos: use the official files from the Design System (Elements → Logos). Use the white or dark-background version on Indigo. Never stretch or recolor a logo.

Alt text

Every image needs a short text description (the “alt text”) for people who can't see it.

  • Describe what the image shows that matters to the page: “Students counting monarch butterflies in a school garden.”
  • Don't start with “Image of” or “Photo of.” Screen readers already announce that it's an image.
  • For a logo, the alt text is the organization's name. For a linked image, describe where the link goes.
  • For a purely decorative image, leave the alt text empty, so that screen readers skip it.

Accessibility checklist

Before you publish, check that:

  • Every text and background pair is rated “Any text” in the combinations table, or “Large text” for headings only.
  • Headings run in order (H1, then H2, then H3), with one H1.
  • Links and buttons say what they do, even when read on their own.
  • Every meaningful image has alt text, and decorative images have empty alt text.
  • No information depends on color alone. Pair color with words, icons or position.
  • Videos have captions, and embedded players have a descriptive title.
  • Tables are used for data, not for laying out the page.
  • The page reads well and nothing is cut off at phone width.
