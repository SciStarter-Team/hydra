Zooniverse and iNaturalist projects

Become a SciStarter Affiliate

Help more people discover your project and give volunteers credit for their participation across projects and websites!

Many volunteers participate in multiple projects on different platforms. SciStarter brings their participation together in one dashboard, helping them track their activities and qualify for certificates, badges, rewards, and course credit when offered through SciStarter programs or instructors.

Why become an Affiliate?

  • Reach more volunteers through SciStarter.
  • Recognize participation across multiple projects and websites in one place.
  • Support learning and rewards by helping volunteers document participation toward certificates, badges, incentives, and course credit.
  • Encourage continued engagement in your project and others.

Zooniverse and iNaturalist

Participation tracking requires two separate opt-ins: project leaders choose to connect their projects, and volunteers independently opt in by adding their iNaturalist or Zooniverse usernames to their SciStarter profiles. Each is a one-time setup: a project is connected once, and a volunteer connects their username once per platform.

Zooniverse Project Leaders

We'll help you connect your project securely.

  1. Add your project to SciStarter at scistarter.org/add-project.
  2. Email your Zooniverse Project ID to info@scistarter.org. This is the number at the end of your Project Builder URL (e.g., zooniverse.org/lab/12345).
  3. Authorize the connection. For security, we'll email you our SciStarter username to add under the Collaborators tab of your Zooniverse project's editing page.
  4. Let us know when it's done. We'll complete the connection and confirm when your project is ready.

Your project stays under your control. SciStarter uses its collaborator access strictly for read-only verification of participation. We do not edit your project, change settings, store or use your project's scientific data, or take any other actions.

iNaturalist Project Leaders

iNaturalist projects connect automatically!

  1. Add your project to SciStarter at scistarter.org/add-project. Select Project, not Event.
  2. Enter your iNaturalist project URL (e.g., https://www.inaturalist.org/projects/your-project) as the Project URL.
  3. That's it! SciStarter recognizes the URL and automatically connects your project. No email or additional authorization is needed.

Already listed? Update your SciStarter listing with your iNaturalist project URL. Projects on other iNaturalist Network sites, such as NaturaLista, work the same way.

How Volunteers Opt In

Connecting your project does not automatically track your volunteers. Each person must opt in separately.

Volunteers give SciStarter permission to recognize their participation by adding their iNaturalist or Zooniverse username to their SciStarter profile, and your project's SciStarter page walks them through it. Zooniverse usernames must be entered exactly as they appear on Zooniverse, including capitalization.

Once connected, SciStarter can recognize eligible participation and display it on their personal dashboards.

What SciStarter tracks:

  • The participant's username
  • The project ID
  • The date and time of each iNaturalist observation or Zooniverse classification

What SciStarter does NOT track: The observations, classifications, or scientific data volunteers submit or analyze.

Good to know: Volunteers connect their username only once per platform. Participation is credited from that point forward (not retroactively) and may take up to 24 hours to appear. All iNaturalist projects qualify; Zooniverse projects must complete the Affiliate connection process.

Spreading the Word

Volunteers connect on SciStarter, so the work on your side is letting them know. Your project's SciStarter page walks them through connecting, so link to it wherever your volunteers will see it:

  • Your Zooniverse project's About page and external links, and a post on its Talk boards
  • Your iNaturalist project's description, and a journal post
  • Newsletters, welcome emails, and the instructions for an event, bioblitz, or class

For example: “Keep track of your contributions to our project alongside your other citizen science: connect your Zooniverse username on SciStarter at scistarter.org/your-project.”

Participation counts from the moment a volunteer connects, so ask volunteers to connect before an event, campaign, or course begins rather than after it.

Questions? Contact info@scistarter.org.

Recording participation

Reporting with email hashes is the recommended way to record user participation for most projects: you send your API key and a cryptographic hash of the user's email address. There is example source code demonstrating this process, available in both HTML formatted and plain text formats.

This process is designed to protect user privacy, while still giving credit to users for their participation. The use of a cryptographic hash of the email address means that SciStarter can not connect that hash to a specific person or a specific email address, unless the person in question is a SciStarter member.

It works by sending an HTTP POST to

https://scistarter.org/api/participation/hashed/PROJECT-SLUG?key=API-KEY

The parameters of the POST, sent in application/x-www-form-urlencoded format, are:

parameter type required default description
hashed string yes The cryptographic hash of the user's email
type collection, classification, or signup yes Describes what general type of participation the user engaged in: data collection, data classification/analysis, or signing up for the project
duration real number How long did the user spend on this participation, in seconds, or if count > 1 the mean time per participation. If this information is not available, please provide an estimate
fidelity real number 0.5 Probability that the contribution is accurate, on a range from [0.0-1.0], or mean probability if count > 1
where GeoJSON point (not a feature, just an object containing "type": "Point" and a "coordinates" attribute) no location Location where the participation took place
when YYYY-mm-ddThh:mm:ss (in UTC) Required if you send reports in batches rather than in real-time current time Time when the participation took place
count integer 1 How many participations does this report describe

The project slug is the identifier used for the project's SciStarter page. If a project's SciStarter URL is https://scistarter.org/an-example then the slug is an-example.

The value of the hashed parameter is calculated in Python 3 as hashlib.sha256(email.lower().encode("utf8")).hexdigest(), or the equivalent operations in other programming languages.

If we can't match the hash to a SciStarter user, we ignore the post, returning a simple success response.

Where it fits in your app

Your app reports at moments it already notices:

  • When a volunteer joins. When someone creates an account with your project or signs up for it, send a report with type signup.
  • When a contribution is saved. Once your server has stored an observation, classification, transcription, or other contribution, send a report with type collection or classification. Reporting after the save, rather than when the volunteer presses Submit, means only the contributions you keep are counted.

Some things to keep in mind:

  • Send reports from your server, not from a web page or a mobile app. Reports carry your API key, and anyone can read a key that is built into a page or an app.
  • Don't make volunteers wait. Send the report in the background, after your app has answered the volunteer. If SciStarter can't be reached, keep the report and try again later, with when set to the time of the contribution. Resend only reports that got no answer: sending one again after SciStarter has answered counts it twice.
  • Busy apps can send fewer reports. Instead of one report per contribution, send one per volunteer per session or per day, with count set to the number of contributions, duration to the average time each one took, and when to the time of the session.
  • Report every volunteer. You don't need to know who has a SciStarter account: reports about anyone else are discarded. Volunteers who join SciStarter later are credited from the day they join, not before, so reporting everyone from the start means nobody is missed.
  • Hash the address the volunteer uses on SciStarter. Credit reaches a volunteer only when their SciStarter account has the same email address you hashed. If volunteers might use a different address with you, add an optional “Email address you use on SciStarter” field to their settings, and hash that address whenever it's filled in. Volunteers can also add the address they use with you to their SciStarter account settings.

When using this integration mechanism, the participant might not realize that the integration is happening, unless you tell them, so it's very important to display some form of notification. Report participation with Link Tokens

What is a Link Token?

A Link Token is a code that a volunteer creates on their SciStarter dashboard and pastes into your app or website. With it, your app can let SciStarter know each time that person takes part in your project, and SciStarter adds the contribution to their dashboard.

Link Tokens are built to protect privacy. You don't need the volunteer's email address or any other personal information, and the token itself says nothing about who they are. The volunteer decides whether to connect, and can disconnect at any time.

Link Tokens are a good fit if you don't collect your volunteers' email addresses, or if you'd rather your volunteers choose for themselves whether to connect. If you do have their email addresses, you can report participation with email hashes instead, with nothing for them to set up.

How it works

  1. The volunteer creates a token. While signed in to SciStarter, they open their dashboard at scistarter.org/dashboard, type a name for the token (your project's name works well, so they'll recognize it later), and click Create a Link Token. The new token appears next to the name.
  2. They give it to your app. They copy the token and paste it into a place your app provides for it, such as a "Connect to SciStarter" field in their account settings.
  3. Your app saves it. Keep the token with that volunteer's account. It doesn't expire, so you only need to ask once.
  4. Your app reports each contribution. When the volunteer takes part, your app sends SciStarter a short report that includes the token (see Sending a report, below). The contribution then appears on their SciStarter dashboard.
  5. The volunteer stays in control. They can delete the token from their dashboard whenever they like. From then on SciStarter refuses reports that carry it, until they create a new token and give it to you.

A volunteer can create as many tokens as they like, each with its own name. Encourage them to make a separate one for each app, so that disconnecting one doesn't affect the others.

What SciStarter receives

Only what your report contains: which project, what kind of participation, and, if you choose to send them, when and where it happened, how long it took, and how many contributions it covers. SciStarter does not receive the observations, classifications, or other data volunteers submit to your project.

Volunteers may not notice the connection at work unless you tell them, so say so in your app. For example: "Each time you submit an observation, we'll let SciStarter know, so it shows up on your SciStarter dashboard."

Where it fits in your app

Your app needs three things: a place for volunteers to connect, a few well-chosen moments to suggest it, and a report each time a connected volunteer takes part.

A place to connect

Add a SciStarter section to the volunteer's account settings, or to your app's settings screen if volunteers don't have accounts with you. It needs:

  • A sentence saying what connecting does, for example: “Connect to SciStarter to see your contributions to our project on your SciStarter dashboard, alongside the rest of your citizen science.”
  • A link to scistarter.org/dashboard, opening in a new tab or the device's browser, where the volunteer creates the token. Volunteers who don't have a SciStarter account yet can make one there first.
  • A field to paste the token into, and a Connect button.
  • Once connected, a line saying so, and a Disconnect button that forgets the token.

When the volunteer presses Connect, send a report with type signup straight away. If SciStarter answers with success, the token works: show the volunteer that they're connected, and their SciStarter dashboard now shows that they joined your project. If SciStarter answers “Provided token is invalid”, ask them to copy the token again.

Moments to suggest it

Connecting should always be optional, never a condition of taking part. Suggest it where volunteers will notice:

  • As an optional step when they join, which they can skip
  • Right after their first contribution: “Want this to count on SciStarter too?”
  • In welcome emails and newsletters, linking to the settings section

Reporting contributions

  • When your app saves a contribution from a connected volunteer, send a report with the volunteer's token. Send nothing for volunteers who haven't connected.
  • Send reports in the background, after your app has answered the volunteer. If SciStarter can't be reached, keep the report and try again later, with when set to the time of the contribution. Resend only reports that got no answer: sending one again after SciStarter has answered counts it twice.
  • Busy apps can send one report per volunteer per session or per day, with count set to the number of contributions, duration to the average time each one took, and when to the time of the session.
  • Send reports from your server if your app has one, since reports carry your API key. An app that reports straight from volunteers' devices should leave the key out, because anyone can read a key that is built into an app; reports without a key are still recorded, marked as unverified.
  • When SciStarter answers “Provided token is invalid”, the volunteer has disconnected on SciStarter. Forget the token, and show the SciStarter section as not connected.

Sending a report

Send an HTTP POST to

https://scistarter.org/api/participation/link-token/PROJECT-SLUG?key=API-KEY

The parameters of the POST, sent in application/x-www-form-urlencoded format, are:

parameter type required default description
token string yes The Link Token the volunteer gave you, exactly as they gave it
type collection, classification, or signup yes Describes what general type of participation the user engaged in: data collection, data classification/analysis, or signing up for the project
duration real number yes How long the volunteer spent on this participation, in seconds, or if count > 1 the mean time per participation. If this information is not available, please provide an estimate
fidelity real number 0.5 Probability that the contribution is accurate, on a range from [0.0-1.0], or mean probability if count > 1
where GeoJSON point (not a feature, just an object containing "type": "Point" and a "coordinates" attribute) no location Location where the participation took place, for example {"type": "Point", "coordinates": [-71.06, 42.36]} (longitude first)
when ISO 8601 date and time, for example 2026-10-30T14:05:00Z Required if you send reports in batches rather than in real-time current time Time when the participation took place. A time without a Z or a UTC offset is read as UTC. A time SciStarter can't read, or one in the future, is recorded as the moment the report arrived
count integer 1 How many participations does this report describe

For example, to report that a volunteer spent five minutes collecting data for the project an-example: 

curl -X POST "https://scistarter.org/api/participation/link-token/an-example?key=YOUR-API-KEY" \
     --data-urlencode "token=THE-VOLUNTEERS-LINK-TOKEN" \
     --data-urlencode "type=collection" \
     --data-urlencode "duration=300" \
     --data-urlencode "when=2026-10-30T14:05:00Z"

A report may carry more than one token parameter, for instance when several volunteers take part together. It is credited to every volunteer whose token SciStarter recognizes.

What SciStarter sends back

status response meaning
200 {"result": "success", "operation": "record"} The contribution was recorded.
400 {"error": "Provided token is invalid"} SciStarter doesn't recognize the token. Usually the volunteer deleted it, or only part of it was pasted. Sending it again won't help: stop using it and ask the volunteer to create a new one.
400 {"error": "The token value is required"} The report had no token.
404 {"error": "Project slug does not identify a project"} Check the project slug in the URL.

A missing or incorrect API key does not cause an error: the report is still recorded, but marked as unverified. Check your key before you go live.

Storing the token

  • Store the token as text, exactly as the volunteer pasted it apart from any spaces before or after it. Capitalization matters. Leave room for at least 64 characters.
  • Ask volunteers to copy and paste the token from their SciStarter dashboard rather than typing it.

Recording participation from a Qualtrics survey

If your project runs on Qualtrics (or a similar form-based survey tool), you can record participation directly from the survey, without writing any code, by having the survey POST the respondent's email address to SciStarter. This turns your survey into a SciStarter affiliate and credits participation to respondents who are SciStarter members. If you're writing your own integration code rather than configuring a survey tool, use the email-hash reporting described above instead.

It works by sending an HTTP POST to

https://scistarter.org/api/participation/email/PROJECT-SLUG?key=API-KEY

Unlike the hashed-email and Link Token integrations, this endpoint accepts the participant's email address in the clear, because the survey collects it directly from the participant. If the email address does not match a SciStarter member, the report is silently discarded and we return a success response, so no participation is recorded for non-members.

Setting up the survey

  1. Add an email question to your survey with a label that makes the integration clear, for example: “Want credit on SciStarter? Enter your SciStarter login email here.” Making participation opt-in and transparent in this way is important — the participant should understand that their email will be shared with SciStarter to credit their contribution.
  2. In Qualtrics, add a Web Service element to your survey flow (placed after the email question), configured to send a POST request to https://scistarter.org/api/participation/email/PROJECT-SLUG?key=API-KEY. Be sure the ?key=API-KEY query parameter is included in the POST URL.
  3. Configure the Web Service to send the value of the email question as the email body parameter (Qualtrics lets you map a piped survey field to the parameter value). Send the body URL-encoded (application/x-www-form-urlencoded), not as JSON.

Where it fits in your survey

  • At the end. Put the Web Service at the end of the survey flow, after the last block of questions, so that a response is credited only when the volunteer finishes the survey.
  • Only when there's an address. Make the email question optional, and place the Web Service inside a Branch that runs only when the email question was answered, so nothing is sent for volunteers who skip it.
  • Matching what the survey is for. Set type to collection for a survey that records observations or other data, or to signup for a registration survey. Leave it out for anything else.
  • For repeated surveys, too. Each completed response counts as one contribution, so a survey that volunteers fill in again and again, such as a monitoring log, credits every response.

Other survey and form tools work the same way, as long as they can send a URL-encoded POST (sometimes called a webhook) when a response is submitted.

The parameters of the POST, sent in application/x-www-form-urlencoded format, are:

parameter type required default description
email string yes The participant's SciStarter login email address, exactly as entered in the survey
type collection, classification, or signup generic participation Describes what general type of participation the user engaged in: data collection, data classification/analysis, or signing up for the project. If omitted, a generic participation event is recorded

When provided, the type parameter must be exactly one of the following values:

  • collection — the participant collected data for the project
  • classification — the participant classified, analyzed, or transcribed data for the project
  • signup — the participant signed up for or joined the project

The project slug is the identifier used for the project's SciStarter page. If a project's SciStarter URL is https://scistarter.org/an-example then the slug is an-example.

Searching for projects

GET https://scistarter.org/p/finder?format=json&q=search words

The answer is a page of up to ten matching projects, in JSON. Combine any of these parameters:

  • q — words to search for
  • lat and lng — a point, in decimal degrees, to sort projects by distance from, nearest first (give both)

  • activity — an activity number, from the list below
  • page — which page of results to return, counting from 1
